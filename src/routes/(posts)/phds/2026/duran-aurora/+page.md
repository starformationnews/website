This thesis investigates the inner structure of the protostellar binary system L1551 IRS 5, a nearby Class I source in Taurus, to understand how disks, envelope, and outflow interact during the early stages of multiple star formation. Using ALMA FAUST observations of 1.3 mm continuum and C18O (2–1) emission, it characterizes the circumbinary and circumstellar disks, constrains the kinematics of the inner envelope, and identifies a centrifugal barrier at about 55 au. The work also develops an analytical model that reproduces the main morphology of the outflow, showing that the observed structure can be explained by a rotating envelope and jet driven cavity expansion. Overall, the thesis provides a coherent physical picture of L1551 IRS 5 across scales from tens to thousands of au, offering new insight into how mass and angular momentum are redistributed in young binary systems.
